# Stop families, footage, and building a chorus

Operating knowledge for drawing stops. Family → colour; footage → pitch;
chorus → how ranks stack; division → how independence is kept.

## The two families and their sub-types

**Flue pipes** (no reed; air-sheet on a lip):

- **Principal / Diapason** — the organ's core tone. Firm, singing, neither
  dull nor bright. The *chorus* stops: principals of several footages plus
  mixtures make the plenum. Every division is built on its principal.
- **Flute** — rounder, fewer upper harmonics; gentle and clear. Stopped
  flutes (Gedackt, Bourdon) are sweet and quiet; open flutes brighter.
  Accompaniment, solo cantabile, quiet undated colour.
- **String** (Gamba, Salicional, Viole) — thin, keen, harmonically rich but
  soft. Warmth and shimmer; blends under a principal or bows a quiet line.

**Reed pipes** (beating tongue; rich, pungent):

- **Chorus reeds** (Trumpet/Trompette 8′, Posaune/Bombarde 16′, Clairon 4′) —
  brilliant, powerful; crown a plenum or make the French *grand jeu*; the
  Pedal Posaune 16′ is the classic bass reed.
- **Solo / colour reeds** (Oboe, Cromorne, Dulzian, Vox Humana) — pungent,
  characterful, *softer*; a solo melody over a flue accompaniment.

## Footage — naming pitch by pipe length

The number is the length (in feet) of the pipe that sounds at written pitch:

| Footage | Sounds | Role |
|--------:|--------|------|
| 16′ | 8ve **below** the key | gravity (manual gravitas; **pedal foundation**) |
| 8′  | at the key (unison) | the **foundation** — the true pitch of the line |
| 4′  | 8ve **above** | first brightener (Octave) |
| 2⅔′ | 12th above (a fifth) | Quint / Nazard — mixture colour, or Sesquialtera |
| 2′  | 15th above (2 8ves) | Super-octave; upperwork brilliance |
| 1⅗′ | 17th above (a third) | Tierce — with 2⅔′ makes the Cornet/Sesquialtera |
| Mixture | several high ranks (octaves & fifths), *breaking* back down | the "silver" crown of the plenum |

Fifth- and third-sounding ranks reinforce the natural harmonics of the 8′, so
the composite reads as one brighter tone, not as added pitches — provided they
are softer than the unison and used inside a chorus.

## Building a chorus (the pyramid)

Add stops **upward by pitch**, each softer than the one below:

- **8′** alone — the plain unison foundation.
- **+ 4′** — the *principal chorus* minimum; brightens without changing pitch.
- **+ 2′ (+ 2⅔′)** — a fuller chorus; the quint adds the mixture's shimmer.
- **+ Mixture** — full **Organo Pleno**; brilliant, festal.
- **+ chorus reed** (Trumpet/Posaune) — the grandest tuttis and cadences.

Rule: the **8′ is loudest, each higher rank progressively softer** (Rule
Priority 2). A top-heavy stack screams; a pyramid rings. Do **not** double a
pitch with two stops of the *same* family and footage unless you want beating
weight — reinforce with *different* footages instead.

## Divisions and the Werkprinzip

A North-German organ is several complete instruments:

- **Hauptwerk / Great** — the largest principal chorus; the plenum lives here.
- **Rückpositiv / Positiv** — a smaller, brighter, more forward chorus; the
  *echo/contrast* manual and home of many solo colours.
- **Brustwerk** — small, delicate, often reedy solos.
- **Pedal** — an **independent** division with its own 16′ (and 32′) principals
  and a 16′ reed (Posaune); it is not a copy of a manual an octave down.

**Couplers** join divisions (Great + Positiv, or a manual to Pedal). Use them
to build the biggest sound — but coupling everything defeats independence, so
prefer terraced contrast (change manuals) for legibility.

## Terracing (sequential design in practice)

Set a level per section; step between levels deliberately:

- **Plenum ↔ Positiv.** A subject/episode on the bright Positiv chorus, the
  answering tutti on the Great pleno.
- **Solo + accompaniment.** A reed or Cornet solo on one manual, quiet flues
  on another, pedal 8′ under.
- **Add the pedal reed** at a structural pillar (a pedal-point, the final
  cadence) for gravity — and rest it where the bass must stay light.

A Buxtehude/Bach praeludium is typically the **Great Organo Pleno with an
independent 16′ pedal**, with terraced relief where the counterpoint thins so
entries can speak — then full pleno + pedal reed for the close.
